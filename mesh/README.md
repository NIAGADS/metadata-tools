# MeSH Annotation of Legacy DSS Studies and Datasets

This folder contains the files and scripts used to back-annotate legacy DSS
studies and datasets with subject headings from the Medical Subject Headings
(MeSH) vocabulary. The annotations support FAIR data principles by making
existing study and dataset records easier to find, access, integrate, and
reuse through consistent, machine-readable subject terms. MeSH terms are
derived by querying publications affiliated with the studies and extracting
the MeSH terms assigned to those publications. The extracted terms are then
reviewed and filtered to exclude broad, geographic, administrative, and other
non-informative concepts that do not add value for NIAGADS cataloging and
discovery.

## Publications MeSH Snapshots

`publications-MeSH-annotations-snapshot.txt` contains a snapshot of MeSH terms
NIAGADS authored or NIAGADS affiliated publications that are not necessarily
affiliated with DSS-hosted data. This review is intended as an extension to
the legacy documentation to help provide a more comprehensive list of
recommended headings for incoming data submissions. These are generated from
parsing the internal cache of publication metadata generated by the revised
NIAGADS main site `/api/publication` route.

> Current Snapshot: 2026-10-07

## Annotation files and interpretation

`study-MeSH-annotations.txt` and `dataset-MeSH-annotations.txt` contain
accession, MeSH term, and MeSH CURIE pairs for DSS studies and datasets. The
terms are inferred from affiliated publications and should be treated as
cataloging recommendations rather than a complete description of the data.
The absence of a term does not necessarily mean that a study or dataset has
no relevant subject matter; it may reflect missing publication links or
publications without MeSH headings.

## MeSH term review files

The three `MeSH-*` files provide the reviewed term lists used to support
cataloging decisions:

* `MeSH-term-review.txt` is the complete review table, with one row for each
  distinct MeSH heading. It records the MeSH term and CURIE, counts from
  publication, dataset, and study annotations, the combined total count, the
  `retain` or `exclude` decision, the reason for that decision, and whether
  further review is needed. This is the detailed audit record of all term
  decisions and their supporting evidence.
* `MeSH-retained-terms.txt` contains the MeSH headings selected for use as
  derived catalog terms.
* `MeSH-recommended-exclusions.txt` contains the headings recommended for
  exclusion, along with their source counts, decision reasons, and any review
  notes needed before finalizing an exclusion.

### Review summary

The review retained 247 MeSH headings and recommended excluding 768, reducing
the candidate term set by 75.7%. Of the recommended exclusions, 73 are flagged
for additional review before finalizing the exclusion decision.

The source counts distinguish terms found in the broader publication snapshot
from terms associated with DSS datasets and studies. Counts help prioritize
terms with direct study or dataset support; they do not by themselves
determine whether a term is retained.

## Submitter support

`suggested-MeSH-headings.html` is a stop-gap support page for distributing
suggested MeSH headings to data submitters before the Data Submission Portal
is released. It provides a searchable list of recommended headings and MeSH
identifiers for use when describing submitted datasets.

## Internal maintenance

`generate_suggested_mesh_headings.py` regenerates the support page from
`MeSH-retained-terms.txt`. It uses only the Python standard library and
preserves the page layout and search behavior while updating the heading rows
and count. This is an internal maintenance utility; data submitters should
use the HTML support page and do not need to run this script. Run it from this
directory with:

```shell
python3 generate_suggested_mesh_headings.py
```

## MeSH term exclusion criteria

MeSH terms extracted from the publication, study, and dataset files were
identified with assistance from ChatGPT (OpenAI) for their usefulness in
NIAGADS topic cataloging, search, and indexing.

A first pass was made to exclude terms that annotated a single (non-study
or non-dataset affiliated) publication.  After that, exclusion decisions
were based on a combination of meaning and frequency, with extra
consideration given to terms annotating publications associated with datasets
and studies.

Recommendations are found in `MeSH-recommended-exclusions.txt`.

Terms were excluded when they described:

* **Generic descriptors:** exclude Humans, Animals, sex, and broad age
  groups; capture these in population metadata.
* **Publication and administrative terms:** exclude headings about publication
  format, funding, organizations, and research management.
* **Generic research processes:** exclude broad terms that do not usefully
  distinguish dataset subjects.
* **Clinical headings:** exclude clinical observations, treatments, procedures,
  outcomes, and other subjects unrelated to genomic data or associated
  phenotypes.
* **Incidental details or downstream findings:** exclude headings describing
  publication-specific experimental details, interpretations, or applications
  rather than deposited data.
* **Excessive specificity:** exclude individual genes, proteins, chromosome
  numbers, and narrow pathways, with explicit exceptions for key AD genes such
  as APOE and TREM2 and markers such as tau and amyloid.
* **Redundant broad headings:** exclude when a retained, more specific heading
  adequately covers the same subject.

**Weight by source:** favor headings supported by study or dataset annotations,
while still applying these exclusions. Publication-only headings require a
clear repository connection; repeated occurrence in affiliated publications
alone is insufficient.
